Something in my room has been ticking for twenty five minutes.
I have not moved. It has not stopped.
First, I open the notebook. The ticking gets louder.
Then I write one word. The ticking gets faster.
Then I look under the desk. It is round. It is red. It is smiling.
Then the bell rings. Twenty five minutes, exactly. I breathe out.
In the eighties, a student named Francesco Cirillo timed his studying with a kitchen timer shaped like a tomato.
Pomodoro is the Italian word for tomato.
Now I take my five minute break. Under the desk, it is already ticking again.
