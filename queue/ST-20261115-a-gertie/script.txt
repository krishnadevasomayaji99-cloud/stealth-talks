A bridge opened in July, and it was dead by November.
Its nickname was Galloping Gertie, because the deck rolled in the wind like a wave.
Then came the seventh of November, with wind of about forty miles an hour.
The road began to twist. One side rose while the other side fell.
A driver climbed out of his car and left it on the deck.
At about eleven in the morning, the main span broke apart and dropped into the water.
Engineers later blamed wind setting a deck that was too light and too flexible swinging.
Every big bridge is now tested as a model in a wind tunnel before it is built.
And the only casualty was a spaniel named Tubby, left behind in the car.
