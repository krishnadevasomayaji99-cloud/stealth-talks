On one night, telegraph machines began sparking with nobody touching them.
Operators got shocks strong enough to throw them back.
Lights moved across the sky, as far south as Cuba.
Some offices reported small fires.
One operator watched a spark jump from his forehead to the machine.
Then the strangest report came from Boston and Portland.
They switched their batteries off, and the line kept working.
For about two hours, messages crossed on the sky's own current.
The cause was a giant solar flare, and the date was September, eighteen fifty nine.
