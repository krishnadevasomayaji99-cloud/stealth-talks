Hiroo Onoda, an Imperial Japanese Army officer sent to Lubang in the Philippines in December 1944, kept fighting after the war ended. History Hit and History.com report he treated leaflets and search parties as tricks, and that he only stood down on 9 March 1974 when his former commander, Yoshimi Taniguchi, travelled to the island and formally relieved him of duty. The same sources say his group killed about 30 islanders over the years. He died on 16 January 2014, aged 91. Sources differ on his age at surrender (51 or 52) and on the exact number of years, so the Reel says almost thirty.
