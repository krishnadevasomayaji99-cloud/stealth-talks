Almost thirty years after the war, one soldier was still fighting it.
He hid in the hills of a Philippine island with a few comrades.
Leaflets said the war was over. He called them tricks.
Search parties came looking. He called them tricks too.
One by one his companions died or gave up.
Then a young traveller found him, but he still would not leave.
He would only stand down on orders from a superior.
So his old commanding officer, by now a bookseller, flew to the island.
Only then did Hiroo Onoda surrender.
