A ship's biscuit from 1784 is still in one piece.

Sailors on Armada-era ships had a theoretical daily ration of 1 lb of biscuit, and Samuel Pepys later specified "good, clean, sweet, sound, well-baked and well-conditioned wheaten biscuit." In practice it was often mouldy, stale or infested with weevils.

One biscuit from 1784 survived: round, perforated, inscribed "This biscuit was given - Miss Blacket at Berwick on Tuesday 13 April 1784", signed "Bewick" (thought to be the wood engraver Thomas Bewick). It stayed in the Bewick family until about 1950 and is now in the Royal Museums Greenwich collection.
