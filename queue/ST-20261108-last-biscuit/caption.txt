A biscuit that outlived its owner by 240 years.

In the Royal Navy, a sailor's daily ration was biscuit. The Spanish Armada era allowance was about a pound a day, and Samuel Pepys later wrote the standard: "good, clean, sweet, sound, well-baked and well-conditioned wheaten biscuit." By the time sailors actually ate it, it was often mouldy, stale or infested with weevils.

One 1784 ship's biscuit survived: a round, perforated, rock-hard disc inscribed as a gift from "Miss Blacket" at Berwick on 13 April 1784, signed "Bewick" (thought to be the wood engraver Thomas Bewick). It stayed in his family until about 1950 and now sits in the Royal Museums Greenwich collection.
