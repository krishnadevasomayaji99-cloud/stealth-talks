A dead officer washed up on a Spanish beach, and the Allies wanted him found.
His papers named him Major William Martin of the Royal Marines.
In his pockets were a fiancée's letters, a ring receipt, and an overdraft notice.
Secret letters said the Allies would invade Greece, and that Sicily was only a decoy.
Spanish authorities passed copies to the Germans.
A single eyelash left in the papers was gone when they came back, so British intelligence knew someone had read them.
Hitler moved forces toward Greece and Sardinia.
When the real invasion hit Sicily, the defences were weakened, and the island fell in thirty eight days, not ninety.
Major Martin never existed. The body was a homeless Welshman who died at thirty four. His name was Glyndwr Michael.
And for decades, his grave carried the wrong name.
