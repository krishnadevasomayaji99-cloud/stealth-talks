He never lived the life on his papers, and it helped win a war.
Operation Mincemeat, 1943: British intelligence let a body carrying fake papers drift ashore at Huelva, Spain, under the identity of "Major William Martin" of the Royal Marines. The letters pointed to Greece, with Sicily as a decoy. Copies reached the Germans, who shifted forces, and when the Allies landed in Sicily in July 1943 the island fell in 38 days against an expected 90. The man was later identified as Glyndwr Michael, a homeless Welshman who died aged 34. Sources differ on his rank, on who first proposed the idea, and on the year his identity was added to his grave (late 1990s).
