Outside CIA headquarters there is a message nobody could read.
Four coded messages on one sculpture. Pure nonsense letters.
In the early nineties, an NSA team cracked three of them.
Six years later, a CIA analyst solved the same three with pencil and paper.
A year after that, a computer scientist named Jim Gillogly made the first public announcement.
The fourth message did not move.
Then a clue was released. Berlin.
Years later, another. Clock.
The sculpture is called Kryptos.
Three messages solved. The fourth resisted codebreakers for decades.
