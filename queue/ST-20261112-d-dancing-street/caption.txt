She stepped into the street and could not stop dancing.

The dancing plague of 1518 began in July in Strasbourg, then part of the Holy Roman Empire, when a woman known as Frau Troffea began to dance in the street. Within about a month, historians estimate that dozens to hundreds of people had joined in. Local physicians ruled out astrological and supernatural causes and blamed "hot blood", and the authorities opened guildhalls, built a wooden stage and hired musicians. What caused it is still debated; theories include stress and mass psychogenic illness. The characters in this animation are original illustrations.
