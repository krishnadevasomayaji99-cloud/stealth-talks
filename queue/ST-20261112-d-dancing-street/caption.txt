She stepped into the street and could not stop dancing.

The dancing plague began in July 1518 in Strasbourg, then part of the Holy Roman Empire, when a woman known as Frau Troffea danced in the street with no music. Within days more than thirty people had joined, and by August estimates range from 50 to 400. Physicians blamed overheated blood, and the council opened guild halls and engaged musicians to help the dancers, which backfired. What caused it is still debated, and whether anyone died is disputed. The characters in this animation are original illustrations.
