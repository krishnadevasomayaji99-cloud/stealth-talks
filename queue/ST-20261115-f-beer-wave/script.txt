On 17 October 1814, a wave hit a London street, and it smelled amazing.
It came from a giant wooden vat that burst around half past five.
The wave knocked down a brewery wall.
A house next door collapsed on a family.
Eight people died, and one of them was four years old.
Sources differ on the size of the vat, three thousand barrels in one account, about a million pints in another.
The vat was full of porter, and the street was a brewery's worst day.
Two days later a jury gave its verdict.
They said the eight died casually, accidentally, and by misfortune.
