A plane ran out of fuel in the air, and the reason was a unit mix-up.
On 23 July 1983, an Air Canada Boeing 767 flying from Montreal to Edmonton lost both engines. Captain Robert Pearson, an experienced glider pilot, and First Officer Maurice Quintal, who knew the former air force base at Gimli, Manitoba, glided the plane to a landing on a runway then used as a drag strip. Nobody was killed. With the plane's fuel gauge system faulty and Canada's metric switch under way, the fuel had been calculated with the wrong units, leaving about half the fuel needed. Sources differ on the exact number of people aboard (about 69 in total) and on what happened to the aircraft afterwards.
