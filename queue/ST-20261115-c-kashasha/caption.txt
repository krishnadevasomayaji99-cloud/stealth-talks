The doctors tested the food, the water and the toxins, and found nothing.
On 30 January 1962, at a mission girls' boarding school in Kashasha, Tanganyika, three girls began laughing and could not stop. In the first wave, 95 of 159 pupils were affected. The school closed on 18 March, the attacks spread to nearby villages and another school, and episodes lasted from hours to 16 days. The investigating doctors, Rankin and Philip, described it as mass hysteria; it is classed today as mass psychogenic illness. Sources disagree on the total affected (often given as about 1,000), the number of schools closed, and how long it lasted.
