In this town, your phone battery is your lifespan.
Mia is twenty. She scrolled all night.
Her battery hit twelve percent. Her knees started to shake.
She ran into the snow, to the old charger by the lake.
Her grandpa sat on the ice, smiling. Three percent.
He had given his charge away for years, a little to everyone.
Mia plugged his phone into hers.
Her number fell. His number climbed.
They hugged in the forest, and both of them glowed.
Then the screen turned to you. Your battery says sixty one percent.
Who will you charge today?
