The deadliest wave in Boston history was not water. It was molasses.

On 15 January 1919, at about 12:30 pm, a tank holding roughly 2.3 million US gallons of molasses burst in Boston's North End. The wave was estimated at 35 mph. 21 people died and about 150 were injured. A court-appointed auditor found the company responsible after it had claimed anarchists blew up the tank, and it paid $628,000 in damages. Salt water from a fireboat was used in the cleanup, and the harbour stayed brown until summer.
