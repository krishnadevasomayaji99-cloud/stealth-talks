In one hot summer, the smell of London drove politicians out of parts of their own building, and the fix took 18 days.
In the summer of 1858, temperatures hit 30 degrees and stayed high for weeks. Curtains and blinds were soaked in chloride of lime to block the smell, and cholera and typhoid spread quickly. The government rushed through a bill proposed by Benjamin Disraeli to fund a new sewer, and it became law in just 18 days. Engineer Joseph Bazalgette's brick tunnels still form the basis of London's sewers today.
