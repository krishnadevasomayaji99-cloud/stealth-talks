One hot summer, a river drove politicians out of Parliament.
Temperatures hit thirty degrees and stayed high for weeks.
Curtains and blinds were soaked in chloride of lime to block the smell.
Cholera and typhoid spread quickly through the city.
So the government rushed through a bill to pay for a new sewer, proposed by Benjamin Disraeli.
It became law in just eighteen days.
Joseph Bazalgette built brick lined tunnels that still form the basis of London's sewers today.
And the curtains? Still soaked in chloride of lime.
