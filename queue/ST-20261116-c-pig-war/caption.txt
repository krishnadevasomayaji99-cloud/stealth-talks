On 15 June 1859, American Lyman Cutlar shot a pig in his garden on San Juan Island; the pig belonged to the Hudson's Bay Company. Brig. Gen. William Harney sent troops, and by the end of August the US had 461 men and 14 cannons on the island. The National Park Service and HistoryLink both say the pig was the only casualty. The two sides shared the island for about 12 years until a 1872 arbitration awarded it to the US. Sources differ slightly on troop counts (64 vs 66 for the first landing) and on whether the Kaiser or a commission ruled, so the Reel says a commission set up by the Kaiser.
