A dead man was put on trial, and the judge did not last the year.
In 897, Pope Stephen VI had the body of his predecessor Formosus disinterred, dressed in papal robes, and propped up on the throne to stand trial. A deacon was appointed to speak for the corpse. Formosus was found guilty of usurping the papacy, his acts were declared null and void, three fingers were cut off and the body was thrown into the Tiber. Within months, Stephen was imprisoned and strangled. The body was later pulled from the river and returned to its tomb. Sources disagree on how long Formosus had been dead.
