# Hook writing: the desire-based hook, adapted for story Reels

Source: a creator talk on "desire-based hooks" (a business-marketing talk about turning views into leads/sales). Its examples and lead counts are the speaker's own claims and unverified here, and its sales-funnel parts do not apply: Stealth Talks sells nothing. The transferable idea, in our words:

**In the first 1-2 seconds, show a relatable character AND the thing they want. Do not state a problem or a topic.**
- Viewers lock on to a desired outcome, then instantly judge whether the character is like them. Anything that makes the character unrelatable (money, privilege, special skill, a strange condition) makes people scroll.
- So: wants must be ordinary and instantly readable; obstacles are what make it funny or tense, not the character's advantages.

## Five hook shapes, translated to story Reels
| Shape | Pattern | Story example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| He/she just did | "<ordinary person> just <achieved odd goal>, with <humble method>" | open on the finished odd result, then show how it happened |
| Looking back (about me / narrator) | "I just <result> using <simple method>" | narrator voice claims a result; animation shows the absurd route |
| If I | "If I wanted <outcome>, I'd <simple method>" | narrator proposes a plan; the animation shows it going wrong |
| To you | "If you're trying to <ordinary want>, watch this" | speaks to a viewer want; use sparingly, it needs a real payoff |
| Can you? | "Is it possible to <ordinary want> with <tiny means>?" | a question the ending actually answers |

## Rules for every hook
1. One sentence: who (ordinary, silhouette stable) + what they want. Visible in frame 1-60 (2 s).
2. No conditions that exclude the viewer. No jargon, no setup, no title card.
3. The hook must be paid off. Never promise something the animation does not deliver.
4. Works muted: the want and the obstacle are readable from action alone; narration adds jokes.
5. Do not reuse an opening from any published Reel; check the ledger and playbook.
6. Record each Reel's hook shape in playbook.md and compare retention (watch time) by shape. Only conclude from the numbers; flag small samples.
